Rhoda Ayele, Lewis Lan and Kaela Godfrey read their vignettes from the precedents report: Rhoda’s “The Profitable Thing / The Other Thing,” on places where a commercial success pays for something quieter; Lewis’s “Elastic / On-Demand Architecture” and “Different Scales, Shared Adaptability,” on small nonprofits whose programs exceed their buildings and on the finances of ten arts and media organizations in New York; Kaela’s “Not Not a Gallery,” on art shown in moving trucks, laundromats and a boat on the Saigon River; and Kaela and Rhoda’s “Funding Radio Across Scales,” on how NPR, WFMU and the Cornell Media Guild pay for themselves. With guest Bree Zucker, in the studio in Ithaca, they and Rodrigo Escandón Cesarman discuss what art institutions and corporations have in common, the ways culture gets funded and why it is hard to talk about money, the grant behind 215 Centre Street, how the students’ projects build in income and shared resources, the exhibition Bree curated in the phone booths of Sixth Avenue in 2020, public art as a tool of gentrification, whether the white cube can be radical again, and what gets lost when a space made by its users is redesigned by an architect.
