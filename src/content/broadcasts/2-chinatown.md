Torben Karl, Qian (Julia) Xiang and Sara Shah read their vignettes from the precedents report: Torben’s fifteen moments in the history of housing in Lower Manhattan, from the Collect Pond to the jail going up on the site of the Tombs; Julia’s “One Name, Many Centers,” on the different waves of migration that made Chinatown; and Sara’s “Performing Chinatown,” on how Chinatown’s architectural image was built as a strategy for economic survival. With guest Calvin Zhong, whose MIT thesis imagined housing in place of the jail, they and Rodrigo Escandón Cesarman discuss self-orientalizing, informality, who gets to decide what Chinatown becomes, the cost of a jail bed against a luxury apartment, and the suspension of disbelief that academic projects ask of their audience. The show includes a clip from the documentary *Chasing Light* and closes with Teresa Teng’s “The Moon Represents My Heart.”
