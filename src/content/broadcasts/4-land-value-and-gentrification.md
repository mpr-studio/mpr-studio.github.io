Sara Shah, Jay Son and Matt Miller read their vignettes from the precedents report: Sara’s “Appreciating Land, Declining Use,” on how Chinatown becomes more valuable as property while it gets harder to sustain as a neighborhood; Jay’s “80 Wooster Street: A Share in SoHo,” on the first artists’ cooperative in SoHo and who got a share of its rising value; and Matt’s “On Re Branding Space in NYC,” on CBGB, which became a John Varvatos store, and the courthouse and jail that became Anthology Film Archives. Rodrigo Escandón Cesarman reads “Making Value, Losing Space,” by Qian (Julia) Xiang, on the concert Montez Press Radio held on its fire escape and the many addresses of David Mancuso’s Loft. After Patti Smith’s “Elegie,” recorded on the last night of CBGB, they discuss who benefits when a neighborhood changes, whether owning is the only way to keep a share of the value a cultural space creates, authenticity and the artist’s place in gentrification, the fashion house Eckhaus Latta in a Chinatown mall, and what an architect can lose when redesigning a space made by the people who use it. The show closes with a report from 219 West on the book *Illegal Living: 80 Wooster Street and the Evolution of SoHo* and with Pat Metheny and Lyle Mays’s “As Falls Wichita, So Falls Wichita Falls,” a classic of the Loft.
